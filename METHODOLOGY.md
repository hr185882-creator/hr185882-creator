# Analytical Methodology

## Operating objective

The objective is to convert uncertain, fragmented, or contested information into an auditable analytical record that preserves provenance, uncertainty, counterarguments, falsifiers, and revision conditions.

## Core workflow

`SCOUT → SOURCEGRID → K-ALIGN → IPR → BLACKGLASS → CRF → COMMAND → AURORA GRID → RECORD LOCK`

### SCOUT

Identifies signals, anomalies, claims, decision questions, and potential changes in baseline conditions.

### SOURCEGRID

Maps source provenance, source dependence, corroboration, contamination risk, and the distinction between original evidence and repetition.

### K-ALIGN

Separates:

- verified fact;
- supported inference;
- forecast;
- speculation;
- unverified claim;
- contradicted claim;
- proposition not established by the available record.

### Inflection Point Research

Tests whether a development represents a structural change rather than temporary volatility, narrative attention, or a local event. The analysis requires a plausible mechanism, persistence, scale, constraints, and reversal conditions.

### BLACKGLASS

Constructs the strongest contrary case. It identifies disconfirming evidence, alternative explanations, hidden assumptions, and the clearest falsifier.

### Constraint-First Forecasting

Moves from evidence to binding constraint, gate state, activation trigger, outcome probability, and revision condition. Forecasts are not accepted merely because a narrative is plausible.

### COMMAND

Arbitrates between competing frames and routes the analysis toward an explicit action state.

### AURORA GRID

Produces the operational synthesis:

- what changed;
- why it matters;
- what it forces next;
- what would falsify the assessment;
- current confidence;
- appropriate action state.

### RECORD LOCK

Preserves the final evidence basis, assumptions, probability, unresolved questions, timestamps, and revision history.

## Source hierarchy

1. Primary records and raw evidence
2. Official and institutional records
3. Strong original reporting
4. Qualified secondary analysis
5. Social media, rumor, anonymous aggregation, and manipulation-prone material

Lower-tier material may generate questions but should not independently establish high-confidence conclusions.

## Confidence language

- **Directional:** evidence supports a working orientation, but important uncertainty remains.
- **High confidence:** multiple strong sources and constraints support the judgment; meaningful uncertainty is still explicit.
- **Verified outcome:** the relevant event or fact has been established by the appropriate record.

## Forecast discipline

Every forecast should include:

- baseline probability;
- time horizon;
- key assumptions;
- binding constraints;
- activation triggers;
- strongest counterargument;
- falsifier;
- revision conditions;
- outcome audit.

## Research boundaries

Association is not culpability. Allegation is not adjudication. Repetition is not corroboration. Prediction-market pricing is evidence of market belief, not proof of underlying reality. AI-generated text is not a source.

## Publication standard

Public releases should disclose authorship, evidence cutoff, limitations, corrections process, privacy boundaries, and the role of AI assistance. Sensitive projects require additional review for defamation, privacy, survivor safety, and unintended identification risk.
