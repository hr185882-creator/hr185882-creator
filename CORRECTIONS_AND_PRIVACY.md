# Corrections, Privacy, and Responsible Publication

## Corrections standard

A correction should be issued when a published statement is materially inaccurate, incomplete in a way that changes its meaning, based on a misread source, or no longer supported by the best available record.

A correction record should include:

- the original statement;
- the corrected statement;
- the date of correction;
- the evidence supporting the change;
- whether the correction changes the overall assessment.

Minor typographical changes do not require a substantive correction entry unless they alter meaning.

## Identity and privacy challenges

A person who believes they have been misidentified, incorrectly categorized, or exposed beyond the public-interest purpose of a project may request review.

A review should evaluate:

- whether the identity match is reliable;
- whether the information is already part of a legitimate public record;
- whether publication creates disproportionate harm;
- whether the individual is a public figure, private person, witness, victim, survivor, family member, or unrelated third party;
- whether redaction, removal, relabeling, or additional context is appropriate.

## Sensitive-person standard

Projects involving abuse, trafficking, violence, minors, medical information, or private individuals require stricter controls. Names, images, contact information, addresses, unique identifiers, and indirect identifying details should not be published merely because they appear in a source document.

## Association versus culpability

Appearance in a contact book, flight log, photograph, donation record, meeting record, or social network does not by itself establish wrongdoing. Products should distinguish:

- documented association;
- allegation;
- corroborated allegation;
- civil finding;
- criminal charge;
- conviction;
- unresolved or unsupported claim.

## Evidence cutoff and versioning

Each substantive research product should display an evidence cutoff date and version. Later evidence should be incorporated through a documented update rather than silently rewriting the earlier record.
